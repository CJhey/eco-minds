An important part of what I've been trying to do here in Murcia is figuring out just how exactly one can harness data to study plants with regards to its possibility of cognition. In order to do that, it was important to get a bit more familiar with the kinds of data that are collected at the lab, and to get better at using tools like R. Now, I'm not particularly good at it, but there is enough of interest there that you can look at experimental design from the perspective of someone looking for insights into the cognitive systems of a plant and try to index anything you get to how the plant handles information.
But of course, experimental design, theory, and data are all things that come hand in hand. What if we think of cognition in plants not as individualized, internal information handling, but as a proper coupling with the environment? I'm not the best person to answer this question (very far from it), but something that comes to mind is that at least some cognitive markers that can be operationalized are those that respond to statistical regularities without being fully repetitive. And that's something I will be looking at rather soon I think!
